# Content strategy — TikTok, and the loop underneath it

> A plan for getting people to play, built around one specific insight: for this
> product, **TikTok's job is not to get downloads. It is to put a phrase in
> someone's head that they will later type into a WhatsApp group.**

---

## 1. The distribution mechanic, before any content

Be clear about how a player actually arrives, because it determines everything
else:

```
One person sees a video
        ↓
One person opens the site and starts a party
        ↓
One person pastes a link into a group chat
        ↓
FIVE MORE PEOPLE ARRIVE, having never seen the video
```

**One viewer is worth six players.** That is an unusually good ratio, and it
means the target of every video is not "a person who wants to play a game" but
**the person in every friend group who is the one who suggests things.** There
is exactly one of them per group, they already know who they are, and they are
the entire audience.

It also means the metric that matters is **shares, not views**. A video with
40k views and 30 shares is worth far less than one with 8k views and 400 shares,
because a share *is* a group chat, and a group chat is six players. Watch the
share rate and effectively ignore the view count.

### The one thing every video must do

The viewer must finish it able to answer: **"what do I send my friends?"**

That is one sentence, with a name and a domain in it. Every other creative
decision is downstream of protecting that sentence.

---

## 2. Positioning

**What this is:** the thing you do when the group chat says "we're bored".

**What it is not:** a game. Selling "play domino online" competes with a
thousand apps that have the same five-star screenshots and a better ad budget.

Sell **the night**, not the game. The product is the hour your friends spend
together; domino is just what was on the table. That's also why the brand is
لمّة and not a game name — see [BRANDING.md](BRANDING.md).

**The competition is not other game apps.** It is:
- The group chat going quiet at 11pm.
- Everyone scrolling separately on a call.
- "Let's play something" → three minutes of arguing → nobody plays.

Name that moment and the product sells itself. Nobody needs a domino app.
Everybody recognises the silence.

---

## 3. Language

**Post primarily in Egyptian Arabic.** Not MSA — nobody talks like that, and
MSA in a casual video reads as an advert. Egyptian is also widely understood
across the Levant and the Gulf, which is roughly the whole addressable market.

**English subtitles, burned in.** TikTok's auto-captions are unreliable on
Arabic and frequently embarrassing. Burned-in subtitles also make the video work
with sound off, which is how a large share of scrolling happens.

**Run a separate English account only once the Arabic one works.** Two accounts
in parallel halves the posting cadence on each, and cadence is the single
biggest lever on this platform.

## 4. The seven formats

These are ordered by how reliably each one converts to a share. Don't start with
format 7 because it's the most fun to make.

### Format 1 — "The group chat" ⭐ start here

15–25 seconds. Screen recording of a WhatsApp-style group chat, typed live.

```
"يلا نعمل حاجة"
"زهقت"
"نلعب إيه؟"
[three seconds of nothing]
[a link is pasted]
[cut to: four people's voices over the game, everyone shouting]
```

**Why this one works best:** it dramatises the exact moment the product solves,
it needs no face and no presenter, it's fast to make, and the payoff is *sound* —
real friends actually laughing, which stops a scroll far more reliably than any
gameplay footage does.

The audio is the whole video. Record a real session and use the real audio. Do
not script it; scripted laughter is instantly detectable and poisons the account.

### Format 2 — "طقّ" (the knock) ⭐ the signature format

10–15 seconds. One clip, one sound, one payoff.

Domino's knock is a physical, audible act — you rap the table because you can't
play, and everyone hears it. It's theatre, it's information, and it's already a
recognisable gesture to the entire target audience.

```
[close on the screen: two tiles left, both dead]
[the knock sound + the table-slam animation]
[cut to reaction: the other three immediately understand what it means]
[end card]
```

**Why this works:** it is a piece of culture the audience already owns. You are
not teaching them a game; you're showing them their game, on their phone. That
is a much shorter distance to travel.

This is also the format most likely to produce a **repeatable series** — and a
series is what turns an account from a set of videos into something people
follow.

### Format 2b — "ممنوع تقول" (the Taboo near-miss) ⭐ added later, and it may beat format 2

8–15 seconds. One clip. No editing beyond a cut.

```
[the card fills the screen: one word, four forbidden words under it]
[audio: someone describing frantically, getting closer and closer]
[the buzzer — hard cut on the sound]
[freeze on the forbidden word they said, circled]
```

**Why this is the strongest format the product has, now that Taboo exists:**

1. **The clip is self-contained.** You don't have to understand the app, the
   room, or the party model. You understand it in two seconds because everyone
   has played this in a living room.
2. **The payoff is a sound, and the sound is a punchline.** Format 2's knock is
   a beat; a buzz landing on someone mid-sentence is a joke with a victim, and
   the victim is a friend.
3. **It is infinitely repeatable without repeating itself**, because the
   content is whatever a real person said. Eighty cards, and the video is never
   about the card.
4. **It shows voice without explaining voice.** The single hardest thing to
   convey about this product is that the talking is inside it. This format
   conveys it without a word of narration, because the entire clip *is* the
   talking.

Run it as a series with a fixed frame: same card treatment, same buzzer sound,
same end card. A fixed frame is what makes a viewer recognise the third one as
yours.

**The near-miss variant is better than the buzz.** A describer who *almost*
says the word, catches themselves, and has to find another route is funnier and
more shareable than one who gets caught — the tension resolves instead of just
stopping. Keep both; lead with the near-miss.

### Format 3 — "The switch" (the actual product differentiator)

12–20 seconds. Screen recording, no commentary.

```
[domino, game ends]
[someone says "نلعب حاجة تانية"]
[tap → tap → a completely different game, same people, voices never stop]
[subtitle: "نفس اللينك. نفس الأوضة."]
```

**Why this matters:** this is the one thing no competitor does, and it is
genuinely hard to convey in a screenshot. Video is the only medium that shows
it, because the point is what *doesn't* happen — no new link, no re-joining, no
gap in the conversation.

Keep it silent-commentary. The absence of friction is the message; talking over
it undercuts it.

### Format 4 — "One rule you're playing wrong"

20–30 seconds. Educational, and the most reliably shareable format that exists
in any game vertical.

```
"عمرك ما هتخسر دومينو تاني لما تعرف الحتة دي"
[show a knock]
"هو طقّ على 3 و5. يعني معندوش 3 ولا 5. خلاص."
[show the reading panel revealing exactly that]
"دلوقتي سيبله الطرفين 3 و5 وهو هيطقّ تاني."
```

**Why this works:** people share things that make them look knowledgeable. A
rules video gets sent to the friend who always loses. It also happens to
showcase the "Reading the table" panel, which is the single most interesting
thing in the domino rebuild and is otherwise invisible in a clip.

There are at least fifteen of these in domino alone, plus Liar's Bar tells and
Codenames clue theory. **This is the deepest content well available and it is
almost free to produce.**

### Format 5 — "POV"

10–15 seconds. Text-on-screen over gameplay.

```
POV: انت اللي دايماً بتقترح نلعب إيه
POV: صاحبك اللي بيكدب وانت عارف
POV: الراجل اللي بيطقّ كل دور
```

**Why:** the lowest production cost of any format, and it's how this audience
already tags each other. The comment section becomes "@ahmed ده انت" — and every
one of those tags is a share by another name.

Cheap to make, so use these to hit cadence on days when nothing else is ready.
Don't build the account on them.

### Format 6 — Real session highlights

30–60 seconds. Genuine reactions, real voices, minimal editing.

Highest ceiling, lowest reliability, most work. Requires actually playing with
funny friends and capturing it. Post when you have one; never plan around one.

## 5. Cadence and the first 90 days

**Consistency beats quality on this platform, by a wide margin.** Four ordinary
videos a week outperforms one excellent one, because the algorithm needs volume
to find your audience and because every post is another sample of a very noisy
distribution.

### Weeks 1–4: find what lands

- **4 posts/week.** Rotate formats 1, 2, 4, 5.
- Same three hashtags every time plus one variable — you're training a
  classifier, so give it a consistent signal.
- **Post between 8pm and 11pm Cairo time.** That is when a group chat is idle
  and the whole product is a response to an idle group chat.
- **Reply to every single comment.** Early comment velocity is heavily weighted,
  and at this stage you have the time.

### Weeks 5–8: double down

- **5 posts/week**, weighted toward whichever format is producing shares.
- Start the series. A named, recurring format (`#1 طقّ`, `#2 طقّ`, …) gives
  people a reason to follow rather than just watch.
- First collaboration attempt — see §7.

### Weeks 9–12: compound

- Maintain 5/week.
- Re-cut your three best performers with different hooks. The same video with a
  new first two seconds is a genuinely new video to the algorithm, and this is
  the highest-leverage thing you can do with limited time.
- Start posting the same content to Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. Zero
  extra production, non-zero extra reach.

### What to measure

| Metric | Why | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Shares per 1k views** | The only metric that maps to players | > 10 |
| Watch-through rate | Tells you if the hook works | > 50% for <20s videos |
| Comments per 1k views | Feeds reach | > 5 |
| Followers | Vanity, mostly | ignore below 5k |
| **Rooms created / day** | The actual outcome | Track from `/api/health` |

**Instrument the funnel before you start posting.** Put a `?s=tiktok` parameter
on the bio link and log it; without it you will be guessing for three months.

## 6. Craft notes

**The first 1.5 seconds decide everything.** Not the first three. Start *inside*
the moment — mid-argument, mid-game, mid-knock. Never open on a logo, never open
on "hi guys", never open on an empty screen.

**Sound is the product.** This is a voice-chat game. Videos with real friends
shouting at each other convert; silent gameplay footage does not. If you can
only fix one thing, fix the audio.

**Vertical, 1080×1920, always.** Never letterbox a horizontal screen recording.
Record the app on a phone in portrait — which is what it's designed for anyway.

**Burn in the subtitles.** Sound-off viewing is the default, and Arabic
auto-captions are bad.

**End card, every video, identical.** 1.5 seconds, logo, domain. Recognition
compounds; cleverness doesn't. Asset: [IMAGE_BRIEFS.md §3.2](IMAGE_BRIEFS.md).

**Say the domain out loud once**, in the middle, not the end. People leave
before the end card. A name said aloud in a video is the thing that survives
into the group chat.

**Never say "link in bio" as the only call to action.** Say the domain. A viewer
who is going to tell four friends about this cannot send them your bio.

## 7. Beyond TikTok

### Reddit — worth doing once, carefully

`r/webgames`, `r/WebGames`, `r/boardgames`, `r/Egypt`, `r/arabs`. One honest
post each: what it is, what's different, a direct link, and *answer every
comment*. This is a one-time source of a few hundred genuinely interested
players and some very good bug reports. Do not post repeatedly; do not post
marketing copy. These communities can smell it and the penalty is permanent.

### Discord servers

Board-game and Arabic-gaming Discords are full of the exact person you want: the
one who organises things. Same rules — participate first, link once, be useful.

### The organic loop that matters more than all of this

Every party creates a link that gets pasted into a group chat. **That link is
your real distribution channel**, and it costs nothing. Two implications:

1. **The OG image is the most valuable marketing asset in the project** — see
   [IMAGE_BRIEFS.md §1.1](IMAGE_BRIEFS.md). It is seen by every invited player,
   in a chat, next to a friend's recommendation, which is the highest-trust
   context an impression can have.
2. **Persistent parties** ([MATCHMAKING.md §3](MATCHMAKING.md)) turn that link
   from disposable into permanent. A `/p/elshella` link gets *pinned* in a group
   chat. That is free, permanent distribution inside exactly the groups you
   want, and it is a better growth investment than a month of posting.

### Creator collaboration

Small Egyptian gaming and comedy creators (10k–100k), not large ones. Ask for
one real session with their friends, not an ad read. Their audience is the group
chat.

The offer that works: "play with your friends for twenty minutes, post whatever
was funny". The offer that doesn't: a script.

## 8. Ninety days of concrete video ideas

Enough to not stall in week three, which is when accounts die.

**"One rule you're playing wrong" (format 4) — 15 videos**
1. A knock tells you two numbers they don't have
2. Why you dump heavy tiles early, not late
3. Your partner's leftovers never count against you
4. Blocking both ends is the strongest move in the game
5. Count the sixes — seven of each number exist
6. Finishing on a double doubles the round (karak)
7. Why the double-six always opens
8. When to hold a double instead of playing it
9. Reading who fed a number and who starved it
10. In Liar's Bar, the pile size changes everything
11. Calling liar on the first play is usually wrong
12. In Codenames, the number is a clue too
13. Why spymasters should give 1s more often
14. The assassin word is the one you're not looking at
15. When to pass in Codenames (almost always earlier than you think)

**"طقّ" (format 2) — 10 videos**
One per satisfying knock, block, or last-tile finish. Pure clip, pure sound.

**"ممنوع تقول" (format 2b) — 20 videos, and this is where I'd spend the effort**
One per Taboo card that produces a good near-miss. Twenty is not ambitious: you
will get several usable clips out of a single evening of real play, because the
game generates them continuously and the good ones are obvious the moment they
happen. Fixed frame, fixed buzzer, fixed end card.

The shot list writes itself from the deck — رمضان, ميكروباص, حلاق, بطيخ,
تيتة, زحمة are all cards where the obvious route is blocked and the second
route is funny. Play the deck, keep the clips, post three a week.

**"The group chat" (format 1) — 10 videos**
Ten different ways the chat goes quiet. Night out cancelled. Everyone home sick.
Ramadan evening. Exam week. Post-match. Winter. A bad day.

**"POV" (format 5) — 15 videos**
One per recognisable person at a table: the one who always knocks, the one who
takes forever, the liar everyone can read, the one who joins late, the one whose
phone dies, the one who leaves winning.

**"The switch" (format 3) — 5 videos**
One per game pair. Domino → Codenames. Liar's Bar → Higher or Lower. Etc.

**Reactive / seasonal**
Ramadan evenings (the single biggest opportunity of the year for a product about
people gathering at night — plan for it), Eid, exam season, a big football match
ending, the first cold night of the year.

## 9. What not to do

- **Don't buy views.** The algorithm punishes engagement that doesn't match
  reach, and you will poison the account's classification permanently.
- **Don't post gameplay with no people in it.** The product is the friends. A
  screen recording of tiles moving is a screensaver.
- **Don't make a trailer.** Nobody watches a trailer for a free browser game.
- **Don't run ads before the organic loop works.** If a video can't get shares
  for free, paying to show it to more people just buys you the same failure at
  scale.
- **Don't build a Discord server yet.** An empty Discord is worse than no
  Discord, and it costs time you should spend posting.
- **Don't chase a trending sound that doesn't fit.** The point of the video is
  one sentence in the viewer's head; a trending sound that doesn't serve that
  sentence gets you views and no shares, which is the failure mode that looks
  like success.
